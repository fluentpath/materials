Present Perfect Continuous

Duration + recent activity

A. The form

subject+ have / has+ been+ verb-ing
  • I have been reading.
  • She has been studying.
  • They have been practising.
I / you / we / they → have been
he / she / it → has been

B. Use 1 — duration

We use the Present Perfect Continuous for an activity that started in the past, continued over a period of time, and is still happening now or has a clear connection to now.

  • Daniel has been practising the guitar for three months.
🎸🎸🎸🎸🎸 →
Past — he startedNow — he still plays

The activity started in the past and continues up to now.

for

How long?
  • for two hours
  • for three weeks
  • for several months
  • for a long time

since

When did it start?
  • since Monday
  • since January
  • since 8 o'clock
  • since last summer

C. Use 2 — recent activity

We also use it for an activity that has recently stopped, when we can still see or feel the result now.

🎨 Emma has been painting ↓ it finished a moment ago ↓ 🖐️ her hands are covered in paint
🏃 Alex has been running ↓ it finished a moment ago ↓ 😮‍💨 he is tired

The activity itself is important, because we can see or feel its result right now.

D. Questions

how long+ have / has+ subject+ been+ verb-ing?
  • How long have you been learning Spanish?
  • How long has Mia been practising?
  • How long have they been working on the project?

Use how long when you want to know the duration.

E. for vs since

for = a period of time

  • for five minutes
  • for two weeks
  • for several years
<──── two weeks ────>

since = a starting point

  • since Monday
  • since 2025
  • since breakfast
Monday ●────────────── now

F. Negative

subject+ haven't / hasn't+ been+ verb-ing
  • I haven't been sleeping well.
  • She hasn't been practising recently.

G. Common time expressions

⏱️for📍since❓how long🕐all morning 📅all week🔄recently✨lately🎯this week

The context tells you which expression fits best — a clock time, a day, or a whole period.

H. Fix it!

❌ She has been study for two hours.
✅ She has been studying for two hours.
❌ I have been practice since Monday.
✅ I have been practising since Monday.
❌ He has been working since three hours.
✅ He has been working for three hours.
  • ✓ Always use the -ing form after been.
  • ✓ Use since with a starting point.
  • ✓ Use for with a period of time.
👩‍🏫 Teacher tips
  • Drill the three chips out loud: have/has → been → verb-ing.
  • Say a time phrase and ask the student to shout "for!" or "since!".
  • Ask the student to turn each example into a how long question.
  • Ask the student to make one negative sentence about a fictional character.

A Busy Week

Reading · 6 questions

Zara lives in a small town with her grandmother and her younger brother, Idris. This week she has been busier than usual.

Since Monday she has been building a model bridge for the school science fair. She has been measuring, cutting and gluing small pieces of wood, and the kitchen table has been covered with tools for four days. "How long have you been working on that bridge?" her grandmother asked this morning. "For about ten hours in total," Zara laughed.

She has also been rehearsing a short speech about her design. Lately she has been recording herself on her phone so that she can listen to her pronunciation. Her voice is a little tired, because she has been speaking out loud all evening.

Idris has been helping too. He has been drawing the diagrams since Tuesday, and his fingers are grey from the pencils.

Recently their grandmother has been growing herbs on the balcony, and she has been looking after them every morning since the spring. This afternoon she has been baking bread, so the whole flat smells wonderful.

Zara has not been resting much, but she is happy. She has been learning something new every day.
